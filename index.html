Understanding the data

Three sources, one spending story

The ABS tracks monthly spending up to 2026. Its survey shows who spends what. Homes Victoria reports local rents.

Important: Survey figures are from 2015–16. Monthly figures run to August 2026. Read them as separate snapshots.

Years of spending data

01Geography

Spending rose everywhere, then stalled

Every state spent more in July. In August, half the country cut back.

The ABS measures spending from bank transaction data. A rise means households spent more than last month. It does not always mean prices rose.

July 2026 was strong everywhere. The Northern Territory led with +2.2%. August then cooled. Victoria, WA, Tasmania and the ACT went backwards.

Where you live shapes the change. But which costs are rising fastest?

02Growth

Services and housing are pulling ahead

Every part of the budget has grown. Some costs grew much faster than others.

Total monthly spending rose 63% in ten years. More households and higher prices both pushed it up. Not every cost kept the same pace.

Services grew faster than goods. Over three decades, housing costs rose six-fold. It is the story of the modern budget.

Costs grow at different speeds. Next, see how city and country compare.

03City and country

Cities spend more, regions drive more

City and country households spend differently. Cities pay more, but not on everything.

Capital city households spend about $325 more each week. Housing, food and recreation make up half that gap.

Regional budgets look different, not just smaller. Transport takes a bigger slice outside the cities. Longer travel distances are one likely reason.

Place matters, but so do people. Age and money worries change everything.

04People

Age and stress reshape the budget

Priorities shift across a lifetime. Money worries force hard choices.

Housing is the top cost until age 55. Education costs peak in middle age. Older budgets lean towards food and health.

Money worries change spending. Households under stress protect essentials. Extras like eating out are cut first.

Every group feels housing costs. So where is rent highest?

05Housing

Location sets the price of a home

Housing is now the biggest weekly cost. Where you live sets the price.

Inner Melbourne and bayside suburbs cost the most. Yarra units rent for $698 a week. Some regional houses rent for less than city units.

Houses and units are shown side by side. Each circle grows with the rent. The zoomed maps show crowded Melbourne suburbs.

Takeaways

The cost of living is not one number

Housing now dominates the budget. Cities spend more, regions drive further. Age and stress reshape priorities. Your costs depend on who and where you are.
