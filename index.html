A snapshot of household spending in Australia

Where does the weekly budget go? It depends on place, age and income. Follow the money from states to suburbs.

Where is spending changing?

Spending does not move evenly across Australia. Each state tells its own story.

Monthly change by state

Where did spending rise or fall?

Every state spent more in July 2026. August told a different story. Four of eight regions spent less. Use the month menu to compare.

Change by category and state

Which costs drove the changes?

Categories rarely move together. August transport jumped in NSW, SA and ACT. Health swung most, from −4% to +4%.

Which costs are growing?

Every part of the budget has grown. Some costs grew much faster than others.

Ten years of spending

Which types of spending grew fastest?

Services spending grew 70% in ten years. Goods grew 58%. Services now outpace goods.

Source: ABS Monthly Household Spending Indicator, August 2016 and August 2026.

Our biggest costs, 1984 to 2016

Which categories take the most money?

Housing ranked third in 1984. It became our biggest cost by 2009–10. Clothing fell from sixth to eleventh.

Source: ABS Household Expenditure Survey, 1984 to 2015–16.

Does where you live matter?

City and country households spend differently. Cities pay more, but not on everything.

Capital city vs rest of state

Where is the city–country gap largest?

Capital city households spend more almost everywhere. Housing shows the biggest gap, at $75 a week. Tobacco is the only exception.

Source: ABS Household Expenditure Survey 2015–16.

How each budget is split

Do city and regional budgets differ?

Pick a city to compare. City households spend more eating out. Most regional households spend more on transport.

Source: ABS Household Expenditure Survey 2015–16.

Who spends differently?

Age and money worries reshape the budget. Priorities shift across a lifetime.

Spending across a lifetime

How do priorities change with age?

Food overtakes housing from age 55. Health climbs from 12th to 5th. Education drops to last by 75.

Source: ABS Household Expenditure Survey 2015–16, Table 10.1.

Spending under financial stress

What do households cut first?

Signs of stress include missing a bill or meal. At four or more signs, extras fall 49%. Essentials fall only 29%.

Source: ABS Household Expenditure Survey 2015–16.

Income left after spending

How much is left each week?

Tasmanians keep the least, just $163 a week. Northern Territory households keep the most, $372. Victorians spend 85% of income.

Source: ABS Household Expenditure Survey 2015–16. NT excludes very remote areas.

How much does housing cost?

Housing is now the biggest weekly cost. Where you live sets the price.

Rent across Victoria

Where are rents highest?

Bayside houses rent for $1,175 a week. East Gippsland houses rent for $480. Hover over a region for its rent.

Source: Homes Victoria rental report, December 2024. Blank areas have no data.

Housing's growing share

Is housing outgrowing the rest of the budget?

Housing took 13% of spending in 1984. By 2015–16 it took 20%. Housing grew faster than total spending.

Source: ABS Household Expenditure Survey, 1984 to 2015–16. Not adjusted for inflation.

The cost of living is not one number

Housing now dominates the budget. Cities spend more, regions drive further. Age and stress reshape priorities. Your costs depend on who and where you are.
